# What the numbers mean

MoreMe's script counts simple things in the user's samples. These are habits, not ideas or facts. Use this page to answer "what does this number mean?" in plain words.

Small samples give rough numbers. Under 1,000 words, a single long email can move them a lot.

## How numbers are shown

- Word counts (sentence and paragraph lengths) are whole numbers.
- Rates "per 1,000 words" have one decimal. For example, 12.5 commas per 1,000 words means about one comma every 80 words.
- Shares are whole percents. 20% of sentences are questions means about one in five.
- "Not measured (too little text)" means there was not enough text to give that number.

## The measures

| Name in the report | What it counts |
|---|---|
| Average sentence length | Words per sentence, on average |
| How much sentence length varies | A bigger number means a mix of short and long sentences |
| A short sentence for you | 1 in 10 of your sentences is this short or shorter |
| A typical sentence for you | Half your sentences are shorter than this, half longer |
| A long sentence for you | 1 in 10 of your sentences is this long or longer |
| Average word length | Letters per word. Plain everyday words are about 4 |
| Average paragraph length | Words per paragraph |
| Commas, semicolons, colons | How often each mark appears, per 1,000 words |
| Exclamation marks, question marks | How often each mark appears, per 1,000 words |
| Ellipsis | Three dots (...) or the single ellipsis character |
| Parentheses | Opening brackets like this: ( |
| Long dashes | The long dash character. Many people never type it |
| Medium dashes | The medium dash character |
| Hyphen used as a dash | A hyphen with spaces around it, or two hyphens |
| Contractions | Words like I'm, don't, we'll. Possessives like "Sam's" are counted too |
| I, me, my | How often you write about yourself |
| We, us, our | How often you write as a group |
| You, your | How often you speak to the reader |
| Sentences that are questions | Share of sentences ending in a question mark |
| Sentences ending in an exclamation mark | Share of sentences |
| Word variety | How often new words appear, from 0 to 1. Higher means fewer repeats |
| Words in ALL CAPS | Words of 2 or more letters in capitals, per 1,000 words |
| Sentences that start lowercase | Share of sentences whose first letter is small |
| Emoji | Emoji characters per 1,000 words |
| Sentence opener variety | How often your sentences start with different words, from 0 to 1 |

## What my-voice uses

The my-voice skill shows 12 lines from these measures in its "Measured profile" table: typical sentence, short and long sentences, typical paragraph, commas, exclamation marks, questions, contractions, I/me/my, you/your, long dashes, emoji, and lowercase starts. The other measures stay in the report.

## Limits

- English only. Other languages give numbers that mean little.
- Sentence splitting follows simple rules, so lists, headings, and signatures can count as short sentences.
- The numbers describe the samples given. Work email and texts to friends can look very different.
