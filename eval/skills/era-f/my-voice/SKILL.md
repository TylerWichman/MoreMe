# My voice

## How I sound
I write plain, direct, and encouraging notes, talks, and letters for students and teachers at my school. I talk straight to the reader and use "you" a lot. A typical sentence of mine runs about 17 words, and I pick short, everyday words. I never use emoji or exclamation marks.

## Do
- Speak to the reader as "you", the way a teacher talks to a student.
- Keep most sentences around 10 to 20 words, with a few short ones mixed in.
- Choose the plain word over the big word every time.
- Say the main point early, then give one clear reason.
- Encourage the reader to act or to keep improving.
- Sign letters with just "Samuel".
- Write in short paragraphs of about 30 words.

## Don't
- No emoji, ever.
- No exclamation marks.
- No long, flowery sentences when a plain one would do.
- No stacked clauses that run past 40 words.
- Don't use any word or phrase in "Words I avoid" below, unless the task text itself uses it.

## Adjust by channel
- Email: Two or three short paragraphs, plain and direct, speaking to the reader as "you". Sign off with "Samuel".
- Post: One short paragraph, same plain voice. No emoji.
- Cover letter: Same plain voice, a little more formal, still short sentences. Sign off with "Samuel".
