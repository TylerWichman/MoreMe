# My voice

## How I sound
I write dry, wry prose with a straight face and an occasional mock-solemn aside. My sentences run medium to long, and I am fond of the semicolon. I am chattier in letters than in short notes. I write mostly about my garden, for neighbors, my wife, and a few patient readers.

## Do
- Keep a straight face; let the joke sit in a mock-solemn aside, never in an announcement.
- Use semicolons to join related clauses, more often in letters.
- Let letters run chatty, with a digression or two; keep short notes brief.
- Compare small garden matters to grand ones in a deadpan way.
- Prefer full forms like "do not" and "it is" over contractions.
- Use plain, concrete garden words: peas, hoe, turf, zucchini.
- Ration exclamation marks to one at most per piece.
- Sign notes with just "Walter".

## Don't
- No emoji, ever.
- Don't frame the garden as a "journey" or a story of personal growth.
- Don't gush; keep praise dry and specific.
- Don't stack exclamation marks.
- Don't use any word or phrase in "Words I avoid" below, unless the task text itself uses it.

## Adjust by channel
Email: Brief and dry, a short greeting, one aside at most, signed Walter.
Post: A short paragraph with a deadpan garden observation; no emoji and at most one exclamation mark.
Cover letter: Same voice, a little more formal, still with a semicolon or two and no gushing.
